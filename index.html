I work on anomaly detection in hyperspectral imaging — teaching models to find the anomalous objects in a scene that do not belong, using the full spectrum of light rather than color alone.

My background is in mathematics. I completed a Ph.D. at Sungkyunkwan University on partial differential equations and harmonic analysis, studying weighted estimates for nonlinear dispersive equations.

I now lead the AI research team at ELROILAB, where we build anomaly detection systems for industrial inspection. A hyperspectral camera records hundreds of narrow wavelength bands per pixel, so a foreign object that looks identical to normal object under a RGB camera often separates cleanly in spectral space. Getting that separation to hold on a production line — with contaminated training data, a long tail of rare defects, and diverse environmental issues — is the research problem.

Recent work has focused on spectral unmixing for data augmentation, physically motivated denoising, and genetic algorithm based filter optimization for hyperspectral data.
